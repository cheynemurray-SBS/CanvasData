What you added in your final comments

46 of you left additional comments at the end of the survey. The overall sentiment favors continuity.

65%endorsed Canvas or asked to keep it (30 of 46). Only one comment rejected Canvas outright.

Support isn't the same as satisfied

Nine of you paired an endorsement with a criticism. The position we heard most often was that Canvas has real limitations, but switching would be worse. We read that as a mandate to fix what's frustrating, not as a reason to stop listening.

Your comments pointed to specific improvements: visual design and customization, AI controls and defaults, file visibility, grading workflow, the Inbox, how Instructure communicates updates, and the quality of course design. Each one appears in the commitments below.

The overall sentiment: faculty favor staying with Canvas.

Taken together, your responses point clearly toward keeping Canvas and investing in making it work better for your teaching. Our role in IT is to bring your voice forward faithfully, and to support you in whichever direction the University takes.

It already meets your needs

89% agree Canvas meets their teaching needs, 88% find it easy to use, and 91% find it reliable. Agreement ranged from 77% in Business and Society to 92% in Education.

The cost of change is the top concern

86% of concern responses named content loss, retraining, or both. Those are real hours taken from teaching, and the disruption would reach students, adjuncts, and online programs too.

Your frustrations are fixable in place

What you asked for is better grading options, more control over course layout, and easier access for students and colleagues. Most of that is training, settings, or policy, not a new platform.

Several of you told us a switch would only make sense with clear, faculty-agreed benefits. That standard is reflected in what we're bringing forward, along with the conditions you raised for any future change: a compelling benefit case, reliable full-fidelity migration of your content, real training and support capacity, and faculty involvement from the start.

A few of you asked whether cost, or a decision already made, was behind this survey. We're sharing these results in full so the faculty perspective is visible to everyone, including those who will make the final decision.

You asked, here's what we'll do

We read every comment and grouped your requests by theme. Each one is matched to the kind of response it needs: support we can offer right away, a policy we need to review with campus partners, or a request we'll take to Instructure, the company behind Canvas.

A note on feature requests. We submit requests to Instructure and advocate for them, but the process is largely one-way. Instructure decides whether and when to build a feature, and rarely shares feedback or a timeline, so we can't promise when, or if, a requested change will arrive.

Access for students, TAs, and colleagues

4 comments, several with strong feeling

Students stuck in add/drop or registration holds lose access to readings in the first weeks. You want to add TAs, co-teaching colleagues, guest visitors, reviewers for tenure letters, and graduate auditors, and to keep some material available after a term ends.

Policy and settings

Why faculty can't add people directly. Course enrollment is protected under FERPA. Giving every instructor open access to add people to their courses would put the University out of compliance, so each addition has to go through a documented request.

Student enrollment belongs to the Registrar. The Registrar governs add/drop and controls when students are pushed into Canvas. IT has no role in that process, so questions about a student's access during add/drop or a registration hold should go to the Registrar.

How to request access

Teaching assistants
Email both canvas_support@redlands.edu and registrar@redlands.edu with the course number and the student's name.
Colleagues, guest visitors, and auditors
Email canvas_support@redlands.edu with the course number and the person you'd like added.

Canvas removes some HTML by design. The panel below explains what's affected and how to work around it.

Workarounds when Canvas strips your HTML (5 strategies)

Why Canvas strips your HTML

  • Canvas only keeps HTML on its approved list. Scripts, <style> blocks, forms, and some attributes are removed when you save, to protect students from malicious code.
  • Nothing is wrong with your course when this happens. It's a security feature, and it applies to every Canvas page.

Work within the editor first

  • Use inline style attributes on elements instead of a <style> block. Most common properties, like color, padding, borders, and backgrounds, are kept.
  • Paste code through the HTML editor (the </> icon) rather than the visual editor, then save and reopen to see what Canvas kept.
  • Keep a master copy of your HTML outside Canvas so nothing is lost if the editor strips part of it.

Host full HTML on GitHub Pages

  • Create a free GitHub account and a new public repository, then upload your page as index.html with any images, CSS, or JavaScript it needs.
  • In the repository's Settings, open Pages and deploy from the main branch. GitHub gives you a secure address like your-username.github.io/your-repo.
  • Open that address in a browser to confirm everything works before embedding it.

Embed it in a Canvas page

  • Edit the Canvas page, open the HTML editor, and paste an iframe that points to your GitHub Pages address.
  • Give the iframe a descriptive title for screen readers, set the width to 100%, and adjust the height until no scrollbar appears.
  • Check the page in Student View and on a phone to make sure it fits.

Know the limits

  • GitHub Pages sites are public on the web. Never put student information, grades, exams, or licensed materials there.
  • Embedded pages can't see Canvas data or grades. They're for content and interactivity only, not for submitting work.
  • If the embedded page loads tools from other sites, they must use https or the browser will block them.
  • Instructional Design can help you build or review an embedded page. Email canvas_support@redlands.edu to set up a time.
Example iframe to paste into the Canvas HTML editor
<iframe src="https://your-username.github.io/your-repo/" title="Interactive unit overview" width="100%" height="600" style="border: 0;" loading="lazy"></iframe>

Files, visibility, and setting up a new term

3 comments

Old exams you unpublished were still reachable through Files. Cleaning up files after a course copy is tedious one by one, and scheduled availability didn't behave as expected for at least one of you.

Support and training, plus a settings review
  • A term-rollover checklist covering selective course import, bulk file cleanup, hiding the Files page from students, and checking with Student View.
  • Drop-in rollover help before each term starts.
  • Review whether new course shells should hide Files from students by default.

We recommend hiding the Files link from students. Guide: how to edit course navigation.

Best practices for files, visibility, and a new term (5 strategies)

Keep sensitive files out of reach

  • Our recommendation: hide the Files link from students. In Settings, open the Navigation tab, drag Files into the hidden list, and save. Students then reach files only through the modules and pages you link them from. How to edit course navigation
  • Unpublishing a quiz or page doesn't hide the files attached to it. Unpublish or restrict the file itself in Files as well.
  • Keep exams and answer keys in their own folder and leave that folder unpublished, so everything inside stays hidden.
  • Hiding the Files link doesn't lock files that are linked from published pages, so check those links too.
  • Better still, keep answer keys out of the course entirely, in your personal Canvas files or University cloud storage.

Make scheduled availability behave

  • Set available-from and available-until dates on the file or folder itself, using Schedule student availability.
  • A file inside an unpublished or restricted folder stays hidden even if the file's own dates have passed. Check the folder settings first.
  • Dates follow your Canvas time zone, so confirm your profile's time zone is set correctly.
  • After setting dates, check what students will see in Student View.

Start the term with less to clean up

  • Import into a fresh, empty course shell rather than one that already has content.
  • Choose Select specific content during import, and bring over only the modules, assignments, and files you plan to use.
  • Use the date-adjustment option during import to shift due dates and availability to the new term in one step.
  • Teaching the same course every term or across several sections? Ask us about a Blueprint course. You maintain one master version, and your updates sync to each section without re-importing. Book a time to talk it through.

Clean up files in bulk

  • In Files, check the boxes next to several files to delete or move them together instead of one at a time.
  • Organize files into folders by unit or type before you copy a course, so next term's cleanup is a matter of deleting a folder.

Check before students arrive

  • Walk through modules, pages, and files in Student View before publishing.
  • Run Validate Links in Content from the course Settings sidebar to catch broken links after an import.
  • Book drop-in rollover help or email canvas_support@redlands.edu before the term starts if anything looks off.

Integrations with the tools you use

7 comments, and 18% disagreed that integrations work well

Publisher textbook content, chat tools, collaboration features closer to Microsoft Teams, a shared whiteboard, and more freedom to connect external tools without waiting on administrative review.

Policy and settings

Why tools still need a review. When a third-party tool connects to Canvas, it can gain access to course content and student records. Cybersecurity threats increasingly target exactly these connections, and a poorly secured tool can expose data the University is legally required to protect. That's why IT keeps administrative oversight of every integration. Our goal is to make that review faster and clearer, not to remove it.

  • Request new tools through our existing integration process, explained in the external connections guide below, so each request is tracked and you know when to expect an answer.
  • Security and privacy review of each tool before it connects, covering what data it can reach and how that data is protected.
  • A public list of tools already approved and connected to Canvas.

The Inbox and Canvas updates

Several comments across the survey

Messages lost when pressing Escape in the Inbox, awkward ways to reach individual students, and changes from Instructure that arrive without warning or clear explanation.

Support and training, plus escalation to Instructure
  • Reproduce the reported Inbox issues, share workarounds, and escalate confirmed bugs to Instructure.
  • A short note before each term summarizing Canvas changes that affect your teaching.
  • Track vendor support problems on your behalf so you don't have to chase them alone.
Best practices for the Inbox and Canvas updates (5 strategies)

Write messages that don't get lost

  • Draft longer messages in a document first, then paste them into the Inbox. Pressing Escape or clicking outside the compose window can close it before you send.
  • After sending, check your Sent folder to confirm the message went out.

Reach the right student, privately

  • In the Inbox, filter by course first, then pick the individual student from the address book.
  • When messaging several students at once, choose the option to send an individual message to each recipient. Each student gets a private copy, and replies come back only to you.
  • To reach students based on their work, use the Gradebook's message option on an assignment. It can target students who haven't submitted or who scored below a set score.
  • Use SpeedGrader comments for feedback on a specific submission, and Announcements for anything the whole class needs.

Make sure messages are seen

  • Set your Canvas notification preferences so Inbox messages also reach your email. Replying to a Canvas notification email sends your reply back through the Inbox.
  • State in your syllabus which channel you'll use and how quickly you respond, and remind students to check their own notification settings in the first week.

Stay ahead of Canvas changes

  • Canvas release notes are published monthly, ahead of changes. You can subscribe in the Instructure Community.
  • Features tagged “Feature Preview” are still in development and can change outside the regular release schedule. Avoid relying on them mid-term.
  • Before troubleshooting an outage, check Instructure’s status page.
  • After a term-start update, open your course in Student View to confirm everything still looks the way you expect.

Report a problem so it gets fixed

  • Email canvas_support@redlands.edu with the course and item, the steps you took, what happened compared with what you expected, the date and time, your browser or device, and a screenshot.
  • Those details let us reproduce the issue quickly and escalate confirmed bugs to Instructure with evidence attached.
